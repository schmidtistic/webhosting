Phase by Phase

Phase 01

Teardown

Demo of the old structure — rotted boards, failing joists, and two decades of deferred maintenance hauled to the curb before a single new piece of lumber arrived.

Phase 02

Leveling & Support

Posts re-set, beams sistered, and everything brought back to level before a single new board went down. The diagonal run would make any low spot obvious, so this had to be right.

Post footings — checking plumb
Long level across the full span
New post hardware
Sister beams added for extra support
Phase 03

Framing

New joists, blocking, and ledger. Diagonal decking transfers loads differently than straight runs — extra blocking at 45° intervals keeps everything from racking over time.

New joist layout on the beams
Mid-span blocking for diagonal load
Ledger bolted to the house
Joist hangers throughout
Frame complete and inspected
Phase 04

Diagonal Boards

Each board cut at 45° and run corner to corner. The longest boards cross the full width of the deck — keeping spacing consistent as they get shorter toward the corners took careful measurement from the start.

First boards — establishing the angle
Pattern taking shape
Halfway — spacing still even
Near the edge — cuts getting shorter
Diagonal field complete
Phase 05

Picture Frame Border

Perimeter boards run parallel to the edge, framing the diagonal field like a mat around a photo — and cleaning up all those angled cuts at the boundary in one clean line.

First border board going in
Corner miters — tight fit
Border cleaning up the diagonal ends
Picture frame fully installed
Phase 06

Built-in Lighting

Low-voltage deck lights planned before the boards went down — wire runs routed through the framing, fixtures surface-mounted through the picture-frame border. Once the boards are in, there's no going back.

Wire runs through the joist bays
Fixture placement marked
Lights installed — first test on
Deck at dusk
Night view — why you plan for this early
Phase 07

Vinyl Fence

Privacy fence to finish the perimeter — posts set in concrete, panels snapped in place. The fence and the deck border share the same sight line, so alignment had to be planned before either went in.
