Home Theater

Always wanted a proper movie theater. Built a raised platform for stadium seating, ran all speaker and power cables through the walls and ceiling, floated a 120" screen on a UST projector, hid every wire, and turned a basement rec room into something that actually sounds and feels like going to the movies.

Project Walkthrough

Phase by Phase

Phase 01

The Before

The room had a great foundation for a theater — large open basement, a dedicated nook for the screen wall, and already a projector and 5.1 surround setup. But it was all flat-floor, no elevation, no stadium seating. The kid lying on the carpet in front of the screen to get the best view tells the story. Wires were everywhere. The projector and screen sat at floor level with no clean solution for the center channel. Something had to be built from scratch to make this a real theater.

Phase 02

Platform Framing

Looked into buying a pre-built theater platform. Everything was either cheap-looking, absurdly expensive, or both. Built from scratch instead. The perimeter frame went down first to lock in the footprint — then the whole structure was lifted onto 4×4 posts and leveled to 14 inches. Joists ran across for floor support. A step was added at the front and the left side so you could get on from either direction. Baseboard molding came out where the platform would land. Cooper helped. His name is in there permanently, written on a joist inside the platform before it was closed up.

Phase 03

Wiring, Insulation & Step Lights

Before closing the platform up, everything had to go inside. Power was run to a receptacle on the front face for the front-row recliners and to the top surface for the second row — so both rows of chairs plug in from directly underneath with nothing showing. RGB deck lights were installed in the step risers and wired to a Shelly relay tied into Home Assistant, set to come on automatically at dusk. The whole cavity was packed with Rockwool to deaden the bass resonance — a platform that vibrates at low frequencies kills the theater experience. The "COOPER ♡" inscription got sealed inside.

Phase 04

Subfloor

Plywood went across the top and down the steps, closing the platform up and giving it a surface to carpet over. A grommet was cut into the top surface — flush, clean — for the power cable pass-through to the second row of seating. The carpet was getting pulled up around the platform edge to eventually wrap cleanly. At this stage the room walls had already been painted dark, which was the first hint of where the whole space was going.

Phase 05

Carpet & Seating

Professionally carpeted — platform surface, steps, and risers. The step deck lights cut through the carpet at the riser face and glow blue at night through the Shelly relay. Chairs went up: a zero-gravity massage chair and two power recliners on the platform, with more seating in front. The goal was floating powered chairs with no visible plugs. The receptacle on the platform top surface means the back row plugs in straight through the floor. Nothing shows.

Phase 06

Black Room, Smart Home & Acoustic Treatment

The platform was done but the room still had glare — the tan walls reflected everything and undermined the whole theater effect. The screen alcove got painted full matte black. Then the back section of the room. Every outlet was swapped to black, every vent cover replaced in black. The old drop-ceiling light was replaced with a low-profile matte black LED bar. A Bambu slatted accent wall went in to add texture and break up the paint — a gang extender was needed to keep the switch plate flush with the surface, and a new black multi-switch panel replaced the old white toggle. The switch panel looks like it belongs there. The three-paddle side is connected only to hot — the actual switching is handled by Shelly relays that command the screen to raise or the smart lights to come on. A physical switch that controls a smart relay is the cleanest solution for guests who don't have the app.

Phase 07

First Movie Night

Deadpool & Wolverine. 120 inches. AWOL Vision 3500 Pro ultra-short-throw projector. Bowers & Wilkins 5.1 through a Denon receiver, Definitive SuperCube for the bass. The center channel sits behind the acoustic fabric bottom of the screen — completely invisible, sounds like it's coming from the picture. The screen rises from the floor on a motorized lift. When it's down, the credenza looks like a professional listening area. The Bambu slatted wall finishes the room. This is done enough for now.

What's Next

Future Vision

The theater works. Other rooms need attention first, and some of this costs real money. But the plan is in place.

Retro Gaming Nook — The small alcove on the other side of the room is already painted black to match. Enough space for one arcade cabinet and one digital pinball machine. A dedicated retro gaming corner that fits the room's aesthetic.

Second Display — A second TV on the wall that can't see the main screen. Normally runs movie posters and coming-soon art. When there's a crowd, it mirrors the main screen so overflow seating doesn't miss anything.

Sconce Lighting — Wall sconces alongside the seating, wired into the Shelly system with a dimmer relay so they don't overpower the screen. More electrical work needed — the right kind.

Poker / Dining Table — The room can accommodate a table that doubles as both. Adds a reason to be in the space even when the screen is down.

Audio Upgrades — The Bowers & Wilkins and Definitive SuperCube are excellent. But this room is good enough now that upgrading the bookshelf speakers and center channel is a real conversation. The foundation is there for a serious listening room.

Build the room. Then earn the room.
